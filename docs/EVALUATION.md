# Evaluation

How the numbers in the README are produced, what they mean, and what they do not show.
Everything here is reproduced by one command: `python tools/evaluate.py` (set `LUA=/path/to/lua` if `lua` is not on the path).

## The benchmark

`benchmarks/<dialect>.lua` is generated by `tools/make_dialect_benchmarks.py` from `data/all_regions.csv`
(Irish pronunciations on English Wiktionary, with their region tags) and committed, so it can be run from a clone.

| Dialect | Rows used | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Connacht | tagged Connacht, Galway, Mayo, Aran, Cois-Fharraige, Connemara, plus untagged rows | 8,034 |
| Munster | tagged Munster, Kerry, Cork, Waterford, plus untagged rows | 4,102 |
| Ulster | tagged Ulster, plus untagged rows | 4,785 |

A word's expected IPA is every accepted variant for that dialect; the engine is correct when its output equals any one.
For Munster and Ulster only words with at least one dialect-tagged transcription are scored (untagged IPA on Wiktionary is
written in the Connacht convention, which would punish correct Munster or Ulster rules). Connacht also keeps untagged-only words.

Earlier versions of this repo reported 6,598 Connacht words from a curated list whose source file was not kept. All 6,598
are inside the 8,034 words above. The 1,436 added words are mostly region-tagged (Aran, Mayo) entries the old list left out;
they are harder, which is why the rules-only Connacht figure here (71.3%) is lower than the 79.5% reported before.

The reference transcriptions come from many Wiktionary contributors and are not consistent on some conventions (sonorant
diacritics, a/ɑ backing, stress on monosyllables). Munster and Ulster scores are capped by that noise, not only by missing rules.

## The three conditions

1. **Rules only** (`--no-lexsubs`): the 17 passes, without the generated exception layer.
2. **Rules + exception layer, in-sample**: `passes/lex_subs_<dialect>.lua` is generated from the errors of condition 1 on
   *all* words, then all words are scored. The layer was fitted to those very words, so this number measures how well the
   errors were memorised, not how well the engine transcribes Irish.
3. **Held-out**: a fixed 20% of the words (a word is "test" when the first byte of its MD5 is below 51) is set aside. A second
   layer is generated from the errors on the other 80% only, and the test words are scored with it. **This is the figure to quote.**

The held-out layer helps by only 0.0 to 0.3 points, which is the expected result: an exception layer corrects the words it
was built from and nothing else.

## What these numbers do not show

- **The passes contain word-specific tables** (function words, monosyllable stress lists, vowel and reduction exceptions;
  roughly 600 entries by a crude count). They were tuned against the whole benchmark over time, including the words now
  held out, so even the held-out figure is somewhat optimistic about unseen words. A fully clean estimate needs those
  tables rebuilt from the train words only, or a new test set the engine has never seen. Neither exists yet.
- **Only single words are benchmarked.** Phrases (`ar an mbord`) are not an evaluation target: behaviour across a word boundary
  is not specified and the output can be wrong.
- **Monosyllable stress** follows an inconsistent convention in the reference data, so stress-sensitive and
  stress-insensitive scores differ by up to 2 points.
- **Dialects are idealised.** Ulster follows Donegal conventions from the sources; Munster merges Kerry, Cork and Waterford.

## Reproducing

```sh
python tools/evaluate.py            # all dialects
python tools/evaluate.py munster    # one dialect
lua bench_run.lua mylabel ulster --split test --no-lexsubs   # a single run
```

`evaluate.py` rewrites `passes/lex_subs_<dialect>.lua`. The committed files are its output; CI regenerates them and fails
if they differ.
